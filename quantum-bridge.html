1. Statement of the Open Problem

Modern theoretical physics rests on two mathematically incompatible pillars. Classical mechanics and general relativity describe physical reality as continuous trajectories and smooth fields evolving on a four-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifold (ℳ, gμν), where geometry dynamically responds to energy-momentum. Attempts to construct a continuous, self-consistent mathematical mapping from classical physics to quantum mechanics—and back from quantum substrates to classical spacetime—encounter four foundational obstructions:

The Correspondence and Metric-Superposition Obstruction: Standard quantization promotes canonical coordinates to non-commuting operators, yet gravity resists perturbative renormalization. The Problem of Time: In canonical approaches to quantum gravity (such as the Wheeler–DeWitt formalism), diffeomorphism invariance forces the total Hamiltonian constraint to annihilate physical states (Ĥ|Ψ⟩ = 0). The resulting universal state is timeless and stationary, creating a stark contradiction with the continuous temporal evolution observed in classical mechanics and everyday physical trajectories.

The Observer-Independent Classicality Problem: Standard decoherence models often rely on arbitrary system-environment partitions or external observers to explain why macroscopic objects and spacetime metrics appear classical rather than remaining in coherent superpositions.

Semiclassical Backreaction Pathologies: Coupling quantum field expectation values to classical geometry via the semiclassical Einstein equations introduces ultraviolet divergences. Absorbing these divergences requires fourth-order curvature-squared counterterms, which generically introduce unphysical higher-derivative degrees of freedom known as Ostrogradsky ghosts, destabilizing the vacuum and allowing runaway solutions.

2. The Unified Effective Hamiltonian Architecture

Base Kinetic and Oscillator Operator (Ĥ0): Represents the unperturbed quantum harmonic oscillator and free kinetic substrate. It defines the baseline energy spectrum and phase-space structure from which standard non-relativistic and relativistic wave mechanics emerge.

Gravitational Self-Energy Operator (Ĥgrav): Encodes mass-density operator interactions and localized gravitational self-energy. Gauge Field Energy Operator (Ĥgauge): Captures Yang–Mills gauge field energy and non-Abelian gauge phase corrections. It ensures local gauge invariance is maintained across curved spacetime boundaries and governs phase accumulation along worldlines.

Cross-Sector Interaction Operator (Ĥint): Mediates nonlinear couplings between the matter oscillator modes, the gravitational self-energy sector, and the gauge fields. This operator is responsible for the non-linear boundary interactions that produce observable micro-scale phase shifts.

2.2 Worldline Gauge Phase Corrections

When a quantum state propagates through a region where momentum operators couple to non-trivial metric and gauge connections, it accumulates a quantum gauge phase correction Δφqgc. In regimes where quantum oscillators intersect strong or sharply varying non-linear gravitational potentials, the residual correction term produces a finite, measurable phase anomaly.

Restricting to Hadamard states guarantees that all observable expectation values preserve microcausality (commuting at spacelike separations) and satisfy the microlocal spectral condition.
